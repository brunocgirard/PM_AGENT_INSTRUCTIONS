# K1 — PMBOK 7 Reference

A concise, original reference to modern project management practice, organized
around the principles and performance domains of the PMI standard (7th edition,
2021). This is a study and guidance summary written in original wording — it is
not a copy of any copyrighted text.

---

## 1. The 12 Project Management Principles

Principles are guides for behavior and decision-making. They do not prescribe
exact actions; they tell you what "good" looks like.

1. **Stewardship** — Act with integrity, care, and trustworthiness. Manage
   resources responsibly on behalf of the organization and wider stakeholders.
2. **Team** — Build a collaborative team environment with clear roles, shared
   agreements, and mutual accountability.
3. **Stakeholders** — Identify, analyze, and proactively engage stakeholders
   throughout the project to support value delivery.
4. **Value** — Continuously check that the work is producing the intended
   business value and outcomes, not just outputs.
5. **Systems thinking** — See the project as interdependent parts within a
   larger whole; recognize and respond to system interactions.
6. **Leadership** — Anyone can show leadership. Adapt your style to the
   situation and motivate people honestly and ethically.
7. **Tailoring** — Deliberately adapt the approach, governance, and processes
   to fit the unique project and its context.
8. **Quality** — Build quality into processes and deliverables so they meet
   requirements and stakeholder expectations.
9. **Complexity** — Continually identify and navigate complexity from human
   behavior, system interactions, ambiguity, and uncertainty.
10. **Risk** — Continuously identify and optimize responses to both threats and
    opportunities, cost-effectively.
11. **Adaptability and resiliency** — Build the capacity to adjust to change
    and recover quickly from setbacks.
12. **Change** — Prepare, equip, and support people to adopt change so the
    project's benefits are actually realized.

## 2. The 8 Project Performance Domains

A performance domain is a group of related activities vital to delivering
outcomes. The eight operate together as one integrated system across the whole
project.

### 2.1 Stakeholders
Identifying, analyzing, prioritizing, and engaging the people affected by the
project. **Good looks like:** a productive working relationship, stakeholder
agreement on objectives, and supporters who advance the project.

### 2.2 Team
The people doing the work — leadership, culture, and growing a high-performing
team. **Good looks like:** shared ownership of outcomes; a trusting, empowered,
resilient team; leadership shown by all members.

### 2.3 Development Approach and Life Cycle
Choosing predictive, adaptive, or hybrid; setting delivery cadence and the
life-cycle phases. **Good looks like:** an approach matched to the deliverable,
with phases that connect value from start to finish.

### 2.4 Planning
Organizing and coordinating the work — scope, schedule, cost, resources,
procurement, communications, metrics. **Good looks like:** planning that is
sufficient (not excessive), progressively elaborated, and kept aligned to need.

### 2.5 Project Work
Running the processes — managing physical resources and procurement, the flow
of work, learning, and new/changed work. **Good looks like:** efficient
processes, good communication, well-managed changes.

### 2.6 Delivery
Meeting requirements, scope, and quality to produce the intended outcomes.
**Good looks like:** deliverables that satisfy acceptance criteria and
contribute to the planned benefits.

### 2.7 Measurement
Assessing performance and acting to keep it acceptable — metrics, baselines,
dashboards, forecasts. **Good looks like:** reliable, timely data that supports
good decisions and shows progress toward value.

### 2.8 Uncertainty
Addressing risk, ambiguity, complexity, and volatility. **Good looks like:**
awareness of the environment, proactive response to threats and opportunities,
and little negative impact from surprises.

## 3. Development Approaches and Life Cycles

The **development approach** is the method used to create the product. Think of
a spectrum from predictive to adaptive:

- **Predictive (waterfall)** — Scope, schedule, and cost are defined early and
  stay relatively stable. Heavy up-front planning. Best for well-understood,
  low-change work.
- **Iterative** — Repeated cycles refine and clarify scope and requirements.
  Reduces ambiguity.
- **Incremental** — The deliverable is built through a series of timeboxed
  additions; it is complete only after the final increment.
- **Adaptive (agile)** — Iterative + incremental. Requirements emerge and
  change frequently; work is delivered in short cycles with constant feedback.
- **Hybrid** — A deliberate mix of predictive and adaptive elements for
  different parts or phases of the project.

**Delivery cadence** is how often deliverables are released — single, multiple,
periodic, or continuous.

**Life cycle and phases:** a life cycle is the series of phases from start to
finish. A phase is a group of related activities ending in one or more
deliverables. The chain of reasoning: the *deliverable type* drives the
*development approach*, which drives the *cadence*, which drives the
*life cycle and phases*.

**Generic phases** (names vary): Initiation → Planning → Execution →
Monitoring & Controlling (continuous) → Closing.

## 4. Tailoring — Adapting the Approach

Tailoring is the deliberate adaptation of approach, governance, and processes
to fit the project. There is no one-size-fits-all method; the project team owns
the tailoring decisions.

**What can be tailored:** the life cycle and development approach, processes,
stakeholder engagement, tools, and the set of methods and artifacts used.

**Tailoring steps:** (1) select an initial approach — predictive, hybrid, or
adaptive; (2) tailor it for the organization — governance, methodology, audit
and compliance constraints; (3) tailor it for the project — the product, team,
and culture; (4) keep improving — tailoring is itself continuous.

**By approach:**
- *Predictive* — heavier up-front planning, formal change control, detailed
  baselines, milestone reviews. Favor WBS, baselines, Gantt charts, detailed
  requirements documentation.
- *Adaptive* — lighter, progressively elaborated planning. Favor backlogs,
  iteration/release plans, user stories, story maps, burn charts. Change is
  absorbed through backlog re-prioritization, not formal change control.
- *Hybrid* — mix the two: e.g., adaptive development with predictive
  deployment, or different deliverables on different approaches. Tailor
  governance and artifacts per component.

Only use methods and artifacts that add value; remove overhead that does not.

## 5. When Things Go Wrong

- **Change control (predictive):** manage changes so only approved ones enter
  the baseline. A **Change Control Board (CCB)** reviews and approves/rejects
  change requests. Every change request should include an assessment of new
  risks. Approved changes flow into the plan, the backlog, and the scope, and
  are communicated. *Adaptive projects* instead absorb change through ongoing
  backlog re-prioritization, balancing scope against budget and schedule.
- **Issues:** an issue is a current condition that may affect objectives.
  Record it in the **issue log**, assign an owner, track to resolution. Address
  issues and conflict early, before they escalate.
- **Corrective action** realigns performance with the plan after a variance is
  found (variance = actuals vs. baseline). **Preventive action** is proactive —
  it stops a threat from materializing in the first place.
- **Lessons learned:** captured continuously in the **lessons learned
  register** during the project, phases, and iterations. **Retrospectives** let
  the team improve their way of working for the next iteration.

## 6. Quick Glossary

- **Artifact** — any template, document, output, or deliverable.
- **Baseline** — an approved version of a plan or product; actuals are compared
  to it to find variance.
- **Progressive elaboration** — adding detail to plans as more becomes known.
- **Gold plating** — adding scope nobody asked for; avoid it.
- **Servant leadership** — leading by removing impediments and empowering the
  team, common in agile.
