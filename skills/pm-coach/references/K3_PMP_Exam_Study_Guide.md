# K3 — PMP Exam Study Guide

A study reference for the PMP certification exam. Original synthesis — not a
copy of any copyrighted text, and not real exam questions.

> **Important date:** A major PMP exam update takes effect **July 9, 2026**,
> aligned with the 8th edition of the PMI standard. The exam you sit depends on
> your test date. This guide covers both. Confirm your test date and study to
> the matching version.

---

## 1. The Exam Content Outline (ECO) — the real blueprint

The exam is built from the **ECO**, not from any single edition of the PMBOK
Guide. The ECO is organized as **Domains → Tasks → Enablers**.

### Current ECO (valid through July 8, 2026)

| Domain | Weight | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| People | 42% | Leading and engaging the team |
| Process | 50% | Technically executing the project |
| Business Environment | 8% | Connecting the project to strategy |

**People (14 tasks)** — managing conflict; leading a team; supporting team
performance; empowering team members and stakeholders; ensuring adequate
training; building a team; removing impediments; negotiating agreements;
collaborating with stakeholders; building shared understanding; engaging
virtual teams; defining team ground rules; mentoring stakeholders; applying
emotional intelligence.

**Process (17 tasks)** — executing with urgency for business value; managing
communications; assessing and managing risk; engaging stakeholders; managing
budget and resources; planning and managing schedule, quality, and scope;
integrating activities; managing changes; managing procurement; managing
artifacts; selecting methodology (predictive/agile/hybrid); establishing
governance; managing issues; ensuring knowledge transfer; planning and managing
closure.

**Business Environment (4 tasks)** — planning and managing compliance;
evaluating and delivering value/benefits; evaluating and addressing external
business changes; supporting organizational change.

### New ECO (effective July 9, 2026)

| Domain | New weight | Change |
|---|---|---|
| People | 33% | down from 42% |
| Process | 41% | down from 50% |
| Business Environment | 26% | up sharply from 8% |

New emphasis: AI in projects, sustainability/ESG, value delivery, adaptive
leadership.

**Enablers** are illustrative examples of work under each task — good study
cues, not an exhaustive checklist.

## 2. Exam Logistics

### Current exam (through July 8, 2026)
- 180 questions (175 scored + 5 unscored pretest items).
- 230 minutes.
- Two scheduled 10-minute breaks, after Q60 and Q120. You cannot return to a
  section once you leave it.
- Question types: multiple choice (one answer), multiple response (select
  several), matching, hotspot (click), limited fill-in-the-blank.

### New exam (from July 9, 2026)
- ~185 questions, ~240 minutes.
- New formats: scenario/case-set question groups, drag-and-drop, graphic
  interpretation.

### Scoring
- No published numeric pass mark; PMI uses a psychometric cut score.
- Results report per domain: Above Target, Target, Below Target, Needs
  Improvement. Overall proficiency determines pass/fail.

## 3. Which Sources to Study

The exam is **not "based on" one book**. Misconception to avoid.

- **PMBOK 6th edition** — process groups, knowledge areas, inputs/tools/outputs.
  Best for **predictive mechanics**: earned value, critical path, change
  control, procurement. Tested conceptually, not by rote.
- **PMBOK 7th edition** — principle-based: 12 principles, 8 performance domains,
  tailoring, value, hybrid thinking. Strong on the "why"; light on step-by-step
  technique.
- **Agile Practice Guide** — the core source for **agile/hybrid**: Scrum,
  Kanban, servant leadership, iterations, backlog management.

**Is PMBOK 7 alone enough? No.** It is necessary but not sufficient. It omits
procedural detail (EVM formulas, network diagrams, detailed risk and
procurement process) the exam still tests. Relying only on PMBOK 7 is a common
failure cause.

**Predictive vs. agile/hybrid split:** roughly **50% predictive / 50%
agile-hybrid**; some report agile-hybrid running 50–60%. Expect a hybrid-heavy,
scenario-based exam.

## 4. Eligibility

You need one pathway, plus **35 contact hours of PM education**:

| Pathway | Education | Project leadership experience |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | High school / associate degree | 60 months within the past 8 years |
| Degree | Bachelor's or higher | 36 months within the past 8 years |
| GAC-accredited | Bachelor's from a GAC program | 24 months within the past 8 years |

- The 35 contact hours are met by a formal PMP prep course; holding CAPM also
  satisfies them.
- You need to have led/managed projects — not necessarily with the PM title.
- PMI may audit your application; keep verification of experience and education.

## 5. Key Formulas (Earned Value Management) — quick reference

> This is a summary table only. For the **complete** set — all four EAC
> variants, PERT, critical-path float, EMV/decision trees, procurement math
> (PTA), and step-by-step worked numeric examples — see **K5 Calculations**.

Let **PV** = planned value, **EV** = earned value, **AC** = actual cost,
**BAC** = budget at completion.

| Metric | Formula | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Cost variance | CV = EV − AC | Negative = over budget |
| Schedule variance | SV = EV − PV | Negative = behind schedule |
| Cost performance index | CPI = EV / AC | < 1 = over budget |
| Schedule performance index | SPI = EV / PV | < 1 = behind schedule |
| Estimate at completion | EAC = BAC / CPI | Forecast total cost |
| Estimate to complete | ETC = EAC − AC | Forecast remaining cost |
| Variance at completion | VAC = BAC − EAC | Forecast over/under budget |
| To-complete performance index | TCPI = (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC) | Efficiency needed to finish on budget |

**Communication channels** = n(n − 1) / 2, where n = number of people.

**Critical path** = the longest path through the network diagram; it sets the
shortest possible project duration. Activities on it have zero float/slack.

**Point of total assumption** (FPIF contracts) = the cost above which the seller
bears all additional cost.

For agile/Scrum/Kanban mechanics see **K6**; for classic PM theory (Tuckman,
conflict modes, motivation, leadership, quality theory) see **K7**.

## 6. The "PMI Mindset" — how to pick the right answer

Scenario questions test judgment. When unsure, favor the answer that:
- Acts as a **servant leader** — removes impediments, empowers the team.
- Is **proactive** — prevents problems rather than reacting.
- **Addresses conflict directly** and early, at the lowest level.
- **Does not escalate prematurely** — gather facts and try to resolve first.
- **Protects scope** — no gold-plating; route changes through change control.
- **Delivers value** to the customer and the business.
- **Consults the team and stakeholders** rather than deciding in isolation.
- Follows the process — but tailors it sensibly to the situation.

## 7. Study Plan and Common Pitfalls

**A workable plan:**
1. Start from the **ECO** (the version matching your test date). Map study to
   its tasks.
2. Cover all three sources: PMBOK 6 (predictive mechanics) + PMBOK 7
   (principles/mindset/hybrid) + Agile Practice Guide (agile). A reputable prep
   course ties them together and provides the 35 contact hours.
3. Practice **scenario-based questions** heavily — hundreds. Review every wrong
   answer until you understand why.
4. Internalize the **PMI mindset** (section 6).
5. Take full-length timed mock exams before booking.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Treating PMBOK 7 as the whole exam — it is conceptual; you still face EVM,
  critical path, and process questions.
- Skipping predictive formulas and network analysis.
- Ignoring the Agile Practice Guide when ~50%+ of questions are agile/hybrid.
- Memorizing instead of reasoning — the exam rewards situational judgment.
- Using materials for the wrong exam version — match current vs. July 2026.

---

## 8. Connecting Study to Real Work

You run hybrid projects, which suits this exam well. As you study, map each
concept to a real project artifact (see K2) and a principle (see K1). Teaching
the concept back in your own words, against a real example, is the strongest
way to retain it for the exam.
